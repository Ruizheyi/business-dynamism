==============================================================================
4. WHAT DO THE NEVER-TREATED STATES ACTUALLY DO?
==============================================================================
I previously asserted they contribute nothing to the gradient
because their treatment variable is identically zero. That was
wrong: dropping them changed the coefficient from +0.048 to
+0.233. They supply no treatment variation, but they do help pin
down the sector-year fixed effects, and that changes the
residualised outcome for everyone else.

  gradient: all 27 states              N= 11231 beta=+0.0481 p=0.766 CI [-0.2724,+0.3708] (-2.3%,+3.2%)
  gradient: 20 adopters only           N=  8320 beta=+0.2327 p=0.209 CI [-0.1198,+0.5790] (-1.0%,+5.1%)
  gradient: balanced window, all states N=  5855 beta=+0.0152 p=0.916 CI [-0.2917,+0.3171] (-2.4%,+2.6%)
  gradient: balanced window, adopters only N=  2944 beta=+0.1480 p=0.414 CI [-0.2101,+0.5407] (-1.8%,+4.6%)

  if these move a lot, the gradient depends on which states help
  estimate the sector-year terms, which is worth saying out loud.
  the aggregate specs are less exposed to this because the
  never-treated states are genuine comparison units there.
